Most students study in ways that feel productive but barely work. Highlighting and re-reading create an illusion of competence. The research points elsewhere.

Active recall

Closing the book and forcing yourself to retrieve information strengthens memory far more than passively reviewing it.

Spaced repetition

Reviewing material at growing intervals fights the forgetting curve. Tools like flashcard apps automate the schedule for you.

Interleaving

Mixing different problem types in one session feels harder — and produces better transfer than blocking one type at a time.

Difficulty is not a sign of failure; it is part of the learning process.

Combine all three: quiz yourself, spread sessions over days, and mix topics. Thirty minutes of this beats three hours of re-reading.
